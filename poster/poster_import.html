Background & rationale

S. boulardii CNCM I-745 is a probiotic yeast used clinically against Clostridioides difficile-associated and antibiotic-associated diarrhoea. Despite decades of clinical use, its strain-specific metabolism has never been mechanistically modelled — clinical dosing has run ahead of a mechanistic understanding of what this strain can and cannot do metabolically inside the gut. We built the first six-layer digital twin of this organism, spanning comparative genomics, a strain-specific genome-scale metabolic model, gut-zone-resolved metabolism, host toxin-kinetics, a machine-learning surrogate for fast querying, and a two-species competition model against C. difficile — testing whether the strain carries a hidden metabolic deficit relative to reference S. cerevisiae, and whether its probiotic dosing behaviour can be predicted rather than assumed.

Layer 1

Comparative genomics

8 S. boulardii assemblies screened, 6 qualifying, benchmarked against the S288C reference (17 sequences, 12.16 Mb, N50 924 kb) and a 63-gene control panel (100% recovered). 21 of 26 testable literature-reported gene absences were confirmed across all 6 assemblies; a 5-gene, 2-copy introgressed cluster was identified at 98.9–100% identity (positions 910,215–942,416).

Layer 2

Strain-specific metabolic model

The Yeast9 consensus model (4,105 reactions, 2,748 metabolites, 1,143 genes) was edited with 14 GPR rule changes (6 reactions deleted, 8 rewritten) reflecting 10 genes absent in this strain. Tested on 12 carbon/nitrogen sources, only trehalose utilisation was lost (growth rate 1.639 → 0 mmol gDW−1 h−1); all 11 other substrates were unchanged, and gene essentiality was unchanged across all 1,143 genes.

Layer 3

Gut-zone metabolism

The model was constrained per gut zone across 2,678 reactions (2,677 GPR-mapped). Zone-specific growth rates were 0.084 h−1 (duodenum), 0.136 h−1 (ileum), and 0.026 h−1 (colon), with 500–520 genes differentially significant per pairwise zone contrast. Gastric-survival bioenergetics, modelled via a proton-pumping cost term, identified a lethal membrane-leak threshold at pH 2.

Layer 4

Host toxin-A kinetics

Bayesian MCMC calibration (affine-invariant sampler, 52,800 posterior draws) of toxin-A proteolysis kinetics gave a median half-life of 0.83 h (95% credible interval 0.58–1.23 h), with 74.6% of degradation attributable to protease activity. Sobol sensitivity analysis of an NF-κB signalling module identified ksup as the dominant parameter (S1 = 0.869, ST = 0.881).

Layer 5

Machine-learning surrogate

A 5-member MLP ensemble and baseline models (random forest, gradient boosting, ridge) were trained on 2,000 simulations and cross-validated on 400 held-out points. Random forest performed best (R² = 0.987 ± 0.009), MLP ensemble R² = 0.977 ± 0.015. The surrogate gives a 53,120× speed-up over the LP solver (7.37 μs vs. 391.6 ms per query, 94% coverage).

Layer 6

Two-species competition

A continuous-flow resource-competition ODE model of S. boulardii vs. C. difficile was run across a 256-condition dosing grid. A minimum effective dose of 0.05 (arbitrary units) achieved complete C. difficile exclusion and 100% toxin-A reduction in 208 of 256 conditions (48 coexistence outcomes); undosed toxin-A burden was 61.1 vs. 0 when dosed.

Key finding — a null result

The strain-specific model is functionally near-identical to the reference consensus model: only 1 of 12 tested carbon/nitrogen sources differs, and gene essentiality is completely unchanged across all 1,143 genes (0 gained, 0 lost).

This is a clinically reassuring null result, not a discovered deficiency — CNCM I-745 carries no major hidden metabolic liability relative to reference S. cerevisiae, beyond its known loss of trehalose utilisation.

Integration — all six layers

Falsifiable predictions

  • Trehalose-free medium should reduce CNCM I-745 growth to zero while leaving the reference strain unaffected.
  • Predicted toxin-A half-life of 0.83 h should be directly testable by an ELISA time-course.
  • The predicted minimum effective dose threshold (0.05, grid units) should be testable in gnotobiotic mouse dosing studies.

Limitations

  • Steady-state flux balance analysis (FBA / pFBA) does not capture dynamic regulation.
  • The NF-κB module is prior-predictive only (n = 3,072 prior draws), not yet calibrated to host expression data.
  • The 8-genome comparative panel limits statistical power (exact McNemar test on 6 paired observations, p = 1.0).
  • The competition model uses idealised continuous-flow kinetics and has not yet been validated in vivo.
